For someone who wants to invest but feels overwhelmed by choosing individual funds and managing an allocation themselves, a robo-advisor offers a middle path between doing it entirely yourself and hiring a traditional human financial advisor.

## What is a robo-advisor?

A robo-advisor is a digital platform that automatically builds and manages an investment portfolio on your behalf, based on your answers to a questionnaire about your goals, timeline, and [risk tolerance](/blog/risk-tolerance-and-asset-allocation/). Rather than picking individual funds yourself, the platform's algorithm selects and manages a diversified portfolio for you, typically using low-cost [index funds and ETFs](/blog/what-is-an-etf/).

## How robo-advisors typically work

1. **You complete a questionnaire** covering your investment goals, timeline, income, and comfort with risk.
2. **The platform recommends (and implements) a portfolio allocation**, typically a mix of stock and bond index funds matched to your profile.
3. **You fund the account**, and the platform handles ongoing investment, including automatically reinvesting dividends.
4. **The platform performs automatic rebalancing** over time, adjusting your holdings back to your target allocation as different investments grow at different rates.
5. **Some platforms offer additional automated features**, like tax-loss harvesting (strategically selling losing investments to offset taxable gains elsewhere).

## Robo-advisors vs. self-directed investing

| | Robo-Advisor | Self-Directed (DIY) |
|---|---|---|
| **Effort required** | Minimal after initial setup | Requires ongoing research and decision-making |
| **Cost** | Typically a small annual advisory fee on top of fund expense ratios | Often just fund [expense ratios](/blog/understanding-expense-ratios/), no advisory fee |
| **Customization** | Limited to the platform's model portfolios | Full control over specific fund choices and allocation |
| **Rebalancing** | Automatic | Manual, unless you set it up yourself |

## Robo-advisors vs. traditional human financial advisors

| | Robo-Advisor | Traditional Advisor |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost** | Generally lower annual fees | Often higher fees, sometimes a percentage of assets under management |
| **Personalization** | Algorithm-driven, based on questionnaire answers | Can account for nuanced, complex personal circumstances a questionnaire might miss |
| **Access to a human** | Limited or none on most platforms | Direct access to a person for questions and complex planning |
| **Minimum investment** | Often low or no minimum | Sometimes requires a substantial minimum portfolio size |

## Who might benefit from a robo-advisor?

- **Beginners who want to start investing but feel unsure about building their own allocation** from scratch.
- **People who value automation** over hands-on control, preferring a "set it and mostly forget it" approach.
- **Those with relatively straightforward financial situations**, without complex needs like business ownership, estate planning, or intricate tax situations that might benefit from a human advisor's judgment.

## Who might be better served by other options

- **People with complex financial situations** — significant assets, business ownership, or intricate tax and estate planning needs — may benefit more from a human advisor's personalized judgment.
- **Hands-on investors who enjoy researching and selecting their own investments** may find a robo-advisor's automation limiting rather than helpful, and might prefer the full control of a [self-managed brokerage account](/blog/what-is-a-brokerage-account/).
- **Extremely cost-sensitive investors** might prefer building their own simple index fund portfolio directly, avoiding even a robo-advisor's modest advisory fee.

## What to check before choosing a robo-advisor

- **Advisory fee**: Usually charged as a small annual percentage of assets managed, on top of the underlying funds' own expense ratios — check both.
- **Minimum investment**: Varies by platform; some have no minimum, others require a modest starting balance.
- **Available account types**: Confirm the platform supports the account type you need (standard taxable account, IRA, etc.).
- **Additional features**: Tax-loss harvesting, automatic rebalancing frequency, and access to human advisors (some platforms offer hybrid tiers) can vary meaningfully between providers.

## Key takeaways

- A robo-advisor automatically builds and manages a diversified investment portfolio based on your stated goals and risk tolerance.
- It sits between fully self-directed investing and hiring a traditional human financial advisor, generally cheaper than the latter and more hands-off than the former.
- It's a reasonable fit for beginners with straightforward financial situations who want automation; those with complex needs may be better served by a human advisor.
